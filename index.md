## 📦 What Is Ninja Ripper?

Every modern game stores its characters, weapons, buildings, and environments as 3D data. Normally, that data is locked away inside the game's files in formats that ordinary software cannot open. Ninja Ripper works differently: instead of cracking open the files, it watches the game while it is running and copies the 3D geometry and textures as they are sent to your graphics card.

The result is a set of files you can open in Blender, 3ds Max, Maya, or any other 3D program you prefer. You get real, usable geometry — not screenshots or flat images.

Because it captures at the moment of rendering, Ninja Ripper works across a wide range of engines, including **Unity** and **Unreal Engine 5**, which power a huge portion of the games released today.

## 🎮 Which Games Work Best

Ninja Ripper 2.10 is designed to work broadly, and it handles **Unity** and **Unreal Engine 5** titles especially well since these engines are extremely common in modern releases. In practice, results vary from game to game.

A few general guidelines that experienced users follow:

- **Single-player games** are the easiest and safest choice for testing.
- **Older titles** often capture more cleanly than brand-new ones.
- **Games with heavy anti-cheat or online requirements** should be avoided entirely.
- **Simple scenes** with fewer objects on screen produce cleaner results than crowded battlefields.

Start with something small and offline. Once you understand how the tool behaves, you can move on to more complex titles.

## 🧩 Tips for Better Results

- **Get close.** Models captured from a distance often come out low-detail or fragmented.
- **Pause if you can.** A still scene captures more reliably than fast motion.
- **Capture in good lighting.** Textures read more clearly when the in-game lighting is even.
- **Take several captures.** Multiple attempts give you more to work with and help fill in gaps.
- **Organize your output.** Rename folders by game and date so you can find things later.
- **Check your files immediately.** Opening a capture right after you make it tells you quickly whether your settings need adjusting.
- **Keep notes.** Writing down which game and which scene worked well saves you from repeating experiments.
